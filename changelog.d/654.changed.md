An aspect crop resamples its window straight from the frame (issue #654).
Every `crop:WxH` cut its window before resizing it, one pixel at a time
through the image crate's RGBA view, and the cut was always RGBA8, so the
resize that followed ran with the alpha step on a second window-sized copy:
on a 2400x1600 JPEG and `crop:400x300` the cut took about 9.5 ms and the
resize of it 6 ms, where the same resize on RGB8 takes under 4. The crop
now hands its window on uncut. An RGB8 frame is resampled through a view
that ends at the window's edges and gains its opaque alpha afterwards; an
RGBA8 frame has the window premultiplied where it sits (the crop owns the
frame); a crop that needs no resample copies the window by rows. In the
pipeline `crop:400x300` of that JPEG went from about 25 ms to under 4 ms,
and the RGBA8 copy of the window (13.6 MB there) and the resize's
premultiplied copy of it are no longer made. This closes the gap between
crop_400x300 and resize_w800 in the 0.9.9 benchmark. No crop holds more
memory than it did: the window is read from the frame only where the frame,
the resize's working rows and the output together are no more than the old
chain held at its peak, and a small window that is enlarged or barely
reduced is still cut first and the frame released. The window, the RGBA8
layout of a crop's result, the debug view and the size checks are as they
were. Responses are unchanged.
