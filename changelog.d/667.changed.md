Animated GIF output prepares its frames on up to two threads (issues #667
and #695). Every frame gets a palette of its own and is compressed by
itself, so the frames are quantized and compressed side by side and then
written in frame order: the file is the bytes one thread writes. On a
4-core VM the encode of a 12-frame 480x320 animation takes 69 ms where it
took 123 ms (1.72x). The thread count is the host's cores, two at most,
never more than the frames, and one for a single frame or an animation
under 64x64 pixels. Two is the most because an animated GIF render takes
one permit of the stills lane, like any still, so its threads share the
cores with the still renders beside it: with four, the encode took 43 ms,
but an 800 px JPEG resize beside one such render took 1.13 to 1.29 times as
long as beside the single-thread encoder. With two it takes 0.98 times as
long, and GIF renders beside it run at 1.76 times the single-thread rate.
The processing deadline is still checked before every frame, on whichever
thread takes it. A frame that fails, a deadline that passes or a panic
stops the other threads before their next frame, and the request gets the
failure of the earliest frame, as before. The prepared frames, the file's
own image data, are held until the last one is ready.
