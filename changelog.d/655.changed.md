A smart crop builds its saliency thumbnail from row sums (issue #655). With
no model configured, `crop:WxH:smart` finds its focal point on a thumbnail
at most 128 pixels on a side, made by the image crate's block mean. The
crate read every source pixel through a generic accessor, one block column
at a time, so shrinking a 2400x1600 frame cost about 5 ms for an analysis
that then takes 0.2 ms. For RGB8 and RGBA8 frames the thumbnail now adds
whole source rows into per-column sums and then adds each block's columns:
the same integers in another order, with the crate's block bounds and
rounding, so the thumbnail, the focal point and the crop window are the
same. That thumbnail went from 5.5 to 1.4 ms on RGB8 and from 4.7 to
1.9 ms on RGBA8, and with the crop change of issue #654
`crop:400x300:smart` of a 2400x1600 JPEG went from about 32 ms to under
6 ms in the pipeline. Other layouts, and a thumbnail the crate would
interpolate, take the crate's path. Responses are unchanged.
