`quality(auto)` scores a JPEG with a `fill` against the image the file holds (issue #713). The encoder flattens transparency over the fill, but the search scored its candidates against the image with its alpha dropped, which shows whatever colors the transparent pixels store: for a cut-out stored over noise, the score measured noise the file never shows and pushed the quality up. The quality pick and every score now read the image flattened over the fill, as the encoder writes it, and the image is flattened once instead of at every encode. Four cut-outs of the functional corpus resized to 200 wide came out 24 to 35% smaller at the same target (`crab-cutout.png`, 6,214 to 4,742 bytes) and a fifth image with alpha 2 bytes smaller, and `X-Crabcrop-Quality` and the `ssimulacra2` reported in meta describe the bytes served. A JPEG with a fill and a fixed quality is the file it was.
