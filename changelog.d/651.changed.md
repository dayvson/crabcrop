A `frame:` request extracts its video frame in about three quarters of the
time it took with issue #647 alone, and in about half the time it took in
0.12.0 (issue #651). The extraction now has FFmpeg store the PNG's rows
instead of deflating them: the argv ends `-vcodec png -compression_level 0
pipe:1`, in place of the `-compression_level 1 -pred up` of issue #647,
which no release shipped. The PNG only carries the frame to crabcrop, which
decodes it at once, so nothing is deflated in FFmpeg and nothing inflated in
crabcrop. On a 2 s 1080p H.264 clip, `frame:poster` up to the decoded frame
takes 275 ms where it took 364 ms with the settings of issue #647 (1.32x),
and the two changes together measure 2.0x against 0.12.0 (583 ms to 286 ms,
in a separate run). The cost is memory: the PNG crabcrop holds until the
request is done is the frame's raw pixels, 6.2 MB for an 8-bit 1080p frame
where it was 2.3 MB, about 25 MB for 4K and 100 MB for 8K, and twice those
sizes for a 10-bit video. The 256 MiB cap on the PNG bounds it as before,
and at most two extractions run at a time. The cap admits the frames it
always has: a frame of more than about 33 million pixels (larger than 8K),
whose pixels stored as 16-bit RGBA would not fit it, keeps the encoder's
defaults and is deflated, as in 0.12.0. And when the run with the setting
fails, hands back more than the cap, or hands back a larger frame than the
probe reported, the run without it follows and its answer is the request's,
so a `CRABCROP_VIDEO_DECODER` that does not take the setting still works, at
the cost of one failed run per extraction. The probe, the frame-size
refusal, the decoder's limits and the deadline are the same. Responses are
unchanged. docs/media.md shows the argv.
