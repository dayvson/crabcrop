The AI image carries the license texts of the two models it bundles, in
`/usr/share/doc/crabcrop/models/`, and `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` names both, with
the SHA-256 `Dockerfile.ai` pins for each, what the projects they come from say
about their licenses, and what those licenses ask of a redistribution (issue
#702). The image redistributed `/models/u2netp.onnx` and `/models/yunet.onnx`
with no license text. The texts are copied byte for byte from the projects the
models come from, at pinned commits. YuNet, from OpenCV Zoo, is under the
MIT License, which the zoo's YuNet directory applies to every file in it. For
u2netp, neither project it comes from says which license covers the file:
U²-Net's repository is under the Apache License 2.0 but names no license for the
trained weights, which it links from outside the repository, and rembg, under
the MIT License, which publishes the ONNX file in its `v0.0.0` release, says
that model weights carry their own licenses, independent of its own. The image
ships both texts, and the notices say that neither project settles which one
applies. The notices template pins each model's checksum, so a model replaced in
`Dockerfile.ai` fails `scripts/check_notices.py` until its license is read
again. `Dockerfile.ai` now copies the texts from the build context, so a build of
it needs the repository as its context, as the release workflow and the compose
recipe give it. Nothing changes for a request.
