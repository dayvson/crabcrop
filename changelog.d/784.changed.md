A step's image is now held to a byte budget as well as the 100 megapixel limit: the 400 MB an 8-bit RGBA image at the pixel limit holds (issue #784). A step allocates its image in the layout of the image it works on, so a 16-bit or float source took two to four times the memory of an 8-bit one at the same size: a 2x2 source resized to 10000x10000 and encoded as JPEG peaked at 707 MB as an 8-bit RGBA PNG, 1.10 GB as a 16-bit RGBA PNG, 1.49 GB as an RGB float TIFF and 1.88 GB as an RGBA float TIFF, for the same JPEG. A resize, `fit-in`, `crop`, the image a `pad` fits, the part of an `overlay(...,width)` that lands (scaled in the overlay's own layout), and the image a blurred `pad` scales to cover its canvas are now refused with `400 unsupported_operation` before they allocate an image past the budget, with a message that names it, such as `requested output 10000x10000 at 8 bytes a pixel exceeds the 400000000 byte limit`. A 16-bit RGBA image reaches the budget at 50 megapixels, a float RGB one at about 33 and a float RGBA one at 25, and an 8-bit image within the pixel limit does not, with one exception: a blurred `pad`'s cover follows the ratio of the canvas's aspect to the image's, not the size of the output, so on an image of an extreme aspect it can pass the budget at any depth and for a small canvas. A 5x1000 RGBA8 strip with `pad:1000x1000:blur`, a one-megapixel output, needs a 1000x200000 cover of 800 MB, and is now refused where it rendered before after building that cover. Every render that is not refused is the same bytes as before, and a DPR client hint steps down from the budget as it does from the pixel limit.
