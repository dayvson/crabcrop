A step's image is now held to a byte budget as well as the 100 megapixel limit: the 400 MB an 8-bit RGBA image at the pixel limit holds (issue #784). A step allocates its image in the layout of the image it works on, so a 16-bit or float source took two to four times the memory of an 8-bit one at the same size: a 2x2 source resized to 10000x10000 and encoded as JPEG peaked at 707 MB as an 8-bit RGBA PNG, 1.10 GB as a 16-bit RGBA PNG, 1.49 GB as an RGB float TIFF and 1.88 GB as an RGBA float TIFF, for the same JPEG. A resize, `fit-in`, `crop`, the image a `pad` fits, the part of an `overlay(...,width)` that lands (scaled in the overlay's own layout), and the image a blurred `pad` scales to cover its canvas are now refused with `400 unsupported_operation` before they allocate an image past the budget, with a message that names it, such as `requested output 10000x10000 at 8 bytes a pixel exceeds the 400000000 byte limit`. A 16-bit RGBA source reaches the budget at 50 megapixels, a float RGB one at about 33 and a float RGBA one at 25, while an 8-bit image of 100 megapixels or fewer never does. Every render inside the budget is the same bytes as before, and a DPR client hint steps down from the budget as it does from the pixel limit.
