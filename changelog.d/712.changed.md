The URL grammar reads every number one way, on the server, in the TypeScript
explainer and in the playground, and `spec/url-signing.md` states the rules
under Argument spellings (issue #712). Every URL that answered before answers
the same, in status and in bytes, except the spellings below, which the docs
already ruled out and no first-party builder writes. A negative zero is now
zero, so `saturation(-0)`, `contrast(-0)`, `exposure(-0)`, `vibrance(-0)`,
`highlights_shadows(-0,0)`, `facepad(-0)`, `focalpoint:-0x-0` and a negative
decimal that rounds to zero, such as `saturation(-1e-50)`, share the cache
entry of their `0` spelling instead of keeping one of their own. They render
the bytes they did, and `/explain` reports the `0` spelling in their canonical
form. These spellings now answer `400`: a percent escape with a sign in it,
such as `%+1` in a `text` label, a class name, an `objects` crop class or a
watermark or overlay URL, which decoded to a control character from U+0000 to
U+000F (`invalid_filters`, or `invalid_crop` in a crop); a `text` color with a
sign, `+fffff` or `+fffffff`, which read as `0fffff`; and an upload expiry with
a sign, as in `PUT /upload/<signature>/exp:+9999999999/<name>`, which answered
`201` and now answers `invalid_expiry`, as the same `exp:` segment always has
in an image URL. A `root:` source path keeps the reading it had.
