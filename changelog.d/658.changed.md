`sharpen` sums its 3x3 kernel a row at a time (issue #658). The in-tree
convolution behind `filters:sharpen(...)` worked out each output sample on
its own, walking the kernel's taps and converting bytes to floats as it
went, so the compiler emitted scalar code for 95% of the filter's work. For
the interior of the frame the sum now runs one tap at a time over a whole
row of running totals, with the three source rows converted once. Every
total still receives its taps in kernel order, as the same float multiplies
and adds, so every byte is the same; the border keeps its per-pixel loop.
`sharpen(1.5)` at 800x533 went from 5.6 to 2.0 ms and at 2400x1600 from
47 to 20 ms, on one thread as before, and with the saturation change of
issue #659 a chain of five filters ending in a sharpen went from 8.6 to
4.5 ms. Responses are unchanged.
