The shared tier no longer treats a bucket's refusals as answers (issue #709). A purge whose credentials lack `s3:DeleteObject` got a `403` for every covered copy's delete, counted it as nothing to delete, and marked its tombstone ended, so once its 15 minutes of grace had passed the copy served again on every replica; a refused read of an entry header did the same. A refused read or delete now leaves the scan unfinished and the tombstone in force, as a bucket that does not answer does. A lookup answered with anything but a success or a `404`, such as the `403` of expired keys, was a silent miss. It now counts in `crabcrop_cache_tier_errors_total{tier="shared"}` and is logged as a `shared storage: the store refused a lookup` line, or `failed a lookup` for a `5xx`, `429` or `408`, that names the status and the bucket's error code (`AccessDenied`, `ExpiredToken`, `SignatureDoesNotMatch`), at most one line every 10 seconds with a count of the rest. Each refused lookup, purge scan read and purge scan delete also counts in the new `crabcrop_cache_shared_refused_total`, with `request="lookup"` or `"purge"`, and its line names the status. An upload's existence check that the upload bucket refused, failed or did not answer read as no object, silently; it still does, but now counts in the new `crabcrop_upload_exists_checks_failed_total` and is logged as an `upload storage:` line at the same rate. A role without `s3:ListBucket` gets a `403` instead of a `404` for an entry that is not there, so with one every miss now counts: grant it. On the disk tier, an inline delete of an entry file that fails, such as an evicted entry's, counts in `crabcrop_cache_tier_errors_total{tier="disk"}` and the new `crabcrop_cache_disk_unlinks_failed_total`, and is logged at the same rate, where it was silent; the file stays on disk until the next boot indexes it again, as before. The new series also counts the entry files the boot scan cannot delete.
