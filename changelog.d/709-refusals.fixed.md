The shared tier no longer treats a bucket's refusals as answers (issue #709). A purge whose credentials lack `s3:DeleteObject` got a `403` for every covered copy's delete, counted it as nothing to delete, and marked its tombstone ended, so once its 15 minutes of grace had passed the copy served again on every replica; a refused read of an entry header did the same. A refused read or delete now leaves the scan unfinished and the tombstone in force, as a bucket that does not answer does. A lookup answered with anything but a success or a `404`, such as the `403` of expired keys, was a silent miss, and so was an upload's existence check that failed. Both now count in `crabcrop_cache_tier_errors_total{tier="shared"}`, and every refusal counts in the new `crabcrop_cache_shared_refused_total`, with `request="lookup"`, `"exists"` or `"purge"`, and is logged as a `shared storage: the store refused ...` line that names the status and, for a lookup, the bucket's error code (`AccessDenied`, `ExpiredToken`, `SignatureDoesNotMatch`), at most one line every 10 seconds with a count of the rest. A role without `s3:ListBucket` gets a `403` instead of a `404` for an entry that is not there, so with one every miss now counts: grant it. On the disk tier, an inline delete of an entry file that fails, such as an evicted entry's, counts in `crabcrop_cache_tier_errors_total{tier="disk"}` and the new `crabcrop_cache_disk_unlinks_failed_total`, and is logged at the same rate, where it was silent; the file stays on disk until the next boot indexes it again, as before.
