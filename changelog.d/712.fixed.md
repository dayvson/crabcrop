The TypeScript explainer, and the playground's copy of it, give the server's
verdict on every spelling (issue #712). A number reads as the server's Rust
type reads it: an unsigned argument refuses `-0`, which they accepted in
`trim`, `noise`, `radius`, `border`, the region filters, `curve`, the
watermark and overlay opacity, `crop-rect:` and `focal:`; a decimal rounds to
the nearest 32-bit float, exactly, before its range applies, so
`blur(512.00001)` and `dpr(1.50000001)` are accepted and `blur(1e-40)`,
`gamma(1e-46)` and `sharpen(1e39)` refused; and `format(webp,method:+6)`,
`method:06` and `sns:+50` are accepted. A class name takes what Rust's
`is_alphanumeric` takes, the Unicode Alphabetic property and the number
categories, so `blurclass(हिंदी)` and a circled letter such as `Ⓐ` are
accepted, and the typed builders `filter.blurClass` and `ops.cropObjects` no
longer throw for them. The explainer also refuses what the server refuses
outside the parse: a remainder that
begins with `crabcrop`, `purge`, `purge-group` or `upload`, a request past 100
operations, and one that names more than 16 distinct watermark and overlay
URLs. `spec/explain-verdicts.json` gains a case for each. The CLI's `explain`
verb gives the same verdicts, `sign` and `seal` refuse a URL past either cap,
and `explain` reads a path whose source has a scheme, such as
`/unsafe/filters:blur(2)/http://x/a.png`, which it reported as missing its
source. `explain`, `purge` and `meta` also keep a bare remainder whole, where
they read `300x200/https://cdn.example.com/2024/a.jpg` as the signature `2024`
over the remainder `a.jpg`, and `page:2/300x200/root:docs/a.pdf` as signed by
`page:2`, and so purged, or fetched the metadata of, another remainder.
