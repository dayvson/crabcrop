Request log lines are written by a thread of their own, in batches (issue
#642). Every image request ended with one `write` of its JSON line to
standard output, on the thread that served it and before its response was
sent. A handler now puts the line in a queue in memory, and one thread,
named `crabcrop-log`, writes what has gathered: the first line after a
pause at once, then at most about once a millisecond, each time in one
write. An operator will notice two things. A request's line reaches
standard output up to about a millisecond after its response, no longer
before it. And a process that is killed (`SIGKILL`, the OOM killer) loses
the lines still queued, the last few milliseconds of them.
`CRABCROP_LOG_SYNC=1` restores the line written before the response, one
write for each request, as in releases through 0.12. Over loopback with 64
keep-alive connections, a memory-tier hit went from 13.4 to 10.2 µs for
each request with standard output a pipe, which is what a container
runtime or a service manager gives a process, and from 10.7 to 10.0 µs
with a file. With one connection it went from 61.8 to 52.6 µs with a
pipe, and with a file nothing measurable changes. Lines keep their text
and their order, the purge, upload and shutdown lines keep their places
among them, and a purge's line and an upload's are still written before
the answer. When nobody reads standard output, the queue takes 8 MiB of
lines; after that a request waits before it is handled, as a request
waited in its write before, and no line is dropped. `/health` and
`/metrics` are still answered meanwhile. A write that fails (a closed
pipe, a full disk) costs the lines it could not write, counted on stderr,
where it used to cut the request off with no answer. A clean shutdown
writes the queue before `shutdown: done`, and waits at most 1 second for
a standard output that does not take it.
