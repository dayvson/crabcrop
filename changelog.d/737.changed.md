A trim after a resize or a fit-in no longer copies the frames of an animated source (issue #737). Before any frame renders, each frame runs the steps ahead of each trim once, so that every frame takes the same cut (issue #465). Since issue #713 a trim that is the chain's first step reads the frames where they are, but a trim after other steps ran them on a copy of each frame. When the chain starts with a resize or a fit-in, each frame is now lent to it: the resize premultiplies the frame where it sits, as the render's own resize does, and writes back the rows that changed, of which a GIF frame or an opaque frame has none. On three 600x600 GIF frames resized to 30x30 and trimmed, the trim added 470,662 bytes of allocations where it added 4,790,662. A trim after any other first step, such as a crop, a pad or a filter, still runs on a copy of each frame. Responses are unchanged: 278 renders, of animated GIF and WebP sources (opaque, with transparency, and under partial alpha) through trims after resizes, fit-ins and other steps, and of PNG output, came out byte for byte as before with metadata stripped and preserved, and so did the upgrade corpus.
