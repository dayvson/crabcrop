`sharpen` and `huerotate` hold to values that mean something (issue #713). `sharpen` adds its strength times each pixel's difference from its neighbors, so from 255 on every pixel that differs by a level is already black or white and the picture stops changing: measured on four corpus images, every whole strength from 255 to 16,449 renders the same pixels. Past that the arithmetic left the range a 32-bit float holds exactly, so `sharpen(100000)` moved 5 to 27% of a photograph's pixels and every pixel of a gradient, `sharpen(100000000)` turned flat areas to noise and `sharpen(3e38)` rendered black. A strength past 255 now sharpens as 255 does. `huerotate` converted its degrees to a float before reducing them, so an angle past a full turn rendered a level apart from the angle it stands for here and there, and `huerotate(2147483647)` was not `huerotate(127)`; the degrees are now reduced modulo 360 first, sign kept, so `huerotate(450)` renders as `huerotate(90)`. Every URL that answered still answers, with the same cache key, and strengths up to 255 and angles from -359 to 359 render as they did.
