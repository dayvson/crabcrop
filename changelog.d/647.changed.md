A `frame:` request extracts its video frame about a third faster (issue
#647). The extraction asks FFmpeg for the frame as a PNG, which crabcrop
decodes at once, and at the PNG encoder's defaults writing it was most of
the run: about 250 of the 360 ms a 1080p frame took. The extraction argv
now ends `-vcodec png -compression_level 1 -pred up pipe:1`. PNG is
lossless at every setting, so the frame is the same pixels, and the PNG is
no larger on video frames (2.28 MB against 2.33 MB for that frame). On a
2 s 1080p H.264 clip, `frame:poster` up to the decoded frame takes 292 ms
where it took 444 ms (1.5x). The external tools' exit is also polled sooner
(50 microseconds at first, doubling to the 5 ms it was), which saves a few
milliseconds on every tool run, PDF pages and video encodes included.
Responses are unchanged. So is what the 256 MiB cap on the PNG admits: a
frame of more than about 33 million pixels (larger than 8K) keeps the
encoder's defaults, because past that size how well the frame compresses
decides whether its PNG fits. And when the run with the new settings fails,
hands back more than the cap, or hands back a larger frame than the probe
reported, the run without them follows and its answer is the request's. So
a `CRABCROP_VIDEO_DECODER` that does not take the two settings still works,
at the cost of one failed run per extraction; one that passes its arguments
through to FFmpeg 6.0 or newer takes them. docs/media.md shows the new argv.
