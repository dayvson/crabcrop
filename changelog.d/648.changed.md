Portrait phone photos turn upright about three times faster (issue #648).
EXIF orientation 6, which a phone writes for a portrait photo, and
orientation 5 still went through the image crate's `rotate90`, which writes
each pixel a whole output row away from the last and misses the cache on
almost every one. They now use a rotation that writes sixteen output rows
at a time, as orientations 2, 3, 4, 7 and 8 already had fast forms. On a
2400x1600 RGB frame the rotation takes 4.1 ms where it took 14.1 ms (3.4x),
on the calling thread, and four at once keep that time. A 2400x1600 JPEG
with orientation 6 and a Display P3 profile decodes, turns and converts in
33.6 ms where it took 45.4 ms (1.35x), and in 27.7 ms (1.64x) with the
in-place color conversion of issue #649. The rotation is a permutation of
the pixels, so responses are unchanged: it matches the image crate for all
eight orientation values on every pixel layout. Frames under four million
samples and 16-bit frames keep the image crate's loop, and the `rotate(90)`
filter is not changed.
