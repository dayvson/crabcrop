An encoder reads a frame that already has its layout where it lies (issue
#665). The encode stage built the 8-bit RGB or RGBA layout an encoder
reads by converting the processed image, and for an image that already was
RGB8 or RGBA8 the conversion copied the whole frame: 11.5 MB for a
2400x1600 JPEG, 43 MB for a 4000x2667 image with alpha. Such an image is
now lent as it is. A 2400x1600 JPEG encode went from 29.6 to 27.6 ms and an
800x533 one from 2.58 to 2.50 ms; with four of the large encodes running at
once the gain is 15%, and every encode holds one frame-sized buffer less.
Gray, 16-bit and float images, and an image with alpha flattened onto a
fill for JPEG, are converted once as before. Responses are unchanged.
