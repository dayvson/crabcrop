The disk tier now charges each entry the blocks and the inode it takes, and a volume that runs out of room no longer stops it (issue #777). An entry was charged its file's length and its index record, but a volume gives every file an inode and at least one block, so a tier of small renders took several times its budget on disk: a 1 MiB budget kept 1,168 entries whose files held 358,576 bytes in 4,832 KiB of blocks. Each file is now charged in whole blocks of its volume, the size `statvfs` reports for the directory at boot (one above 64 KiB, as NFS reports its transfer size, counts as 4 KiB), plus 512 bytes for its inode and directory entry, so an entry costs about 5 KiB at least and a 512 MB budget holds about 100,000 entries at most. A tier that fits its budget today may hold fewer entries after the upgrade: the first boot deletes the oldest entries until the rest fit, as it does after a budget is lowered. A volume can still run out of inodes or blocks first, as a default ext4 volume, with one inode for every 16 KiB, does under small renders. Every later write then failed with `No space left on device` and nothing was evicted, since the budget was not full, so the tier stored nothing new. A write that finds no room on the volume, or over its quota, now evicts the oldest entries and lowers the budget to what the tier held, until the next start, so the next write lands. Write failures printed one stderr line each; they are now logged at most once every 10 seconds with a count, and a line for a full volume names the lowered budget. A boot on a volume with no room left for its probe file exited with `the result storage directory ... is not writable: No space left on device`, and since the volume persists, a pod restarted into the same exit until the directory was emptied by hand. The boot now reads the entries, deletes the oldest until the probe file can be written, and serves the rest; a read-only mount or a permission error still stops it. docs/deployment.md and the Helm, kustomize and compose READMEs say how many inodes a volume needs for its budget.
