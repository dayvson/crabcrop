AVIF decodes and SVG renders in the worker process are faster on Linux with
glibc (issue #652). A worker lives for one job, so every byte it touches is
new to it, and at glibc's malloc defaults each frame-sized buffer was mapped
on its own, faulted in 4 KiB at a time and unmapped when freed. The server
now starts a worker with `GLIBC_TUNABLES` in its otherwise cleared
environment. Both jobs get `glibc.malloc.hugetlb=1`, which asks for huge
pages; the kernel grants them where its transparent huge page mode is
`madvise`. An SVG job also gets `mmap_threshold` 32 MiB, `trim_threshold`
256 MiB and `top_pad` 64 MiB, which keep freed memory in the worker's heap
for its short life. On a 4-core host with glibc 2.39 and huge pages in
`madvise` mode a 2400x1600 AVIF decodes in 48 ms where it took 54 ms
(1.11x), and a 43 KB SVG illustration rasterizes at 800x600 in 54 ms where
it took 62 ms (1.11x; 6% to 11% in four measurements). Where huge pages are
off (`never`) an AVIF decode runs as it did and an SVG render keeps the
thresholds' part of the gain (between 3% and 12% in four measurements). An
AVIF job is not given the thresholds, because without huge pages they slowed
that decode by 2% to 8%. The cost depends on the host: with huge pages a
fault maps 2 MiB at a time, so a worker's resident memory can round up by a
few MiB per large buffer, and where the kernel's huge page defrag mode
compacts memory on a fault, a fault can stall when memory is fragmented. The
worker's 4 GiB address-space cap, its CPU limit, its kill at the deadline
and one job per worker are the same: the settings change how the heap is
laid out inside the cap, a job that allocates past it still fails alone, and
the largest jobs the bounds allow stay far below it (an 8192x8192 AVIF with
alpha needs 1,135 MiB of address space where it needed 1,166 MiB, a heavy
4096 px SVG render 993 MiB where it needed 930 MiB). The server's own
`GLIBC_TUNABLES` is not passed on, and nothing changes with musl or on
macOS, where the variable is not set. Responses are unchanged.
