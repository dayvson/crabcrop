AVIF sources come back from the decode worker faster (issue #650). The
worker hands the decoded pixels to the server through a pipe, 15 MB for a
2400x1600 image, and through the default 64 KiB pipe the two processes
traded a wake-up for every 64 KiB: about a fifth of the decode's time. On
Linux the server now asks for a 256 KiB pipe for an AVIF job's answer, and
for its request when the source is larger than 64 KiB. The answer also
comes as two frames, a nine-byte header and then the pixels, so the server
reads the pixels straight into the image's buffer instead of moving all of
them to drop the header. A 2400x1600 AVIF decodes in 46 ms where it took
53 ms (1.13x). SVG jobs keep the default pipes: their rasters are smaller,
the render is most of their time, and the wider pipe measured no gain. The
resize is best effort: where the kernel refuses it, and on other systems,
the pipe keeps its size and the decode runs as before. An unprivileged
user may hold 64 MiB of pipe buffers before the kernel shrinks new pipes
(`fs.pipe-user-pages-soft`); an AVIF decode holds at most 512 KiB of them
while it runs. The worker's address-space and CPU limits, its kill at the
deadline, one job per worker and the decode limits are the same, and an
image whose header announces more pixels than the decode budget is refused
before any of them is read. Responses are unchanged.
