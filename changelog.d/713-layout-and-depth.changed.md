Aspect crops, the redaction filters, and watermarks and overlays placed off the canvas keep the source's layout and depth (issue #713). Each wrote its image back as 8-bit RGBA whatever the source was: `crop:400x200/filters:format(png)` of an opaque JPEG came out an RGBA PNG where `400x250` came out RGB, `crop:32x32` of a 16-bit PNG came out 8-bit where a resize kept 16 bits, and `blur_region` on an opaque JPEG served as PNG carried an alpha channel, as did a watermark that drew nothing. Now an 8-bit RGB source stays RGB, a gray one stays gray, a 16-bit source stays 16-bit and a float one keeps its samples until the encoder converts them; an image has alpha only when the source has it or an operation adds real transparency. The redactions (`blur_region`, `pixelate_region`, `blurfaces`, `pixelatefaces`, `blurclass`, `pixelateclass` and `recolorclass`) keep every sample outside the redacted box exactly, and blur, pixelate and blend inside it at the image's own depth; `recolorclass` paints color, so it turns a gray image into color at the same depth. An 8-bit source's colors are the ones the RGBA8 form had, to the byte: rendering 50 sources (the 38 of the functional corpus and 12 16-bit, float, gray and progressive ones) through 41 operation chains before and after the change, an opaque source's crop or redaction keeps its RGB exactly, its PNG drops the alpha channel and comes out 2 to 17% smaller, 12% at the median of 168 such renders (a 160x100 crop of `crab-photo.jpg` went from 39,703 to 35,703 bytes), and its JPEG XL drops the alpha channel too, while its JPEG, WebP, AVIF and GIF files are the bytes they were. An RGBA8 source renders as before. A 16-bit or float source's crop is resized at its own depth, so its pixels change by the rounding the trip through 8 bits made, and its PNG keeps 16 bits. A watermark or overlay that lands on the image still composites in 8-bit RGBA, as before.
