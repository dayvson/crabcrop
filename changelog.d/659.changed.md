`grayscale` and `saturation` work on sixteen pixels at a time (issue #659).
Both mapped one pixel at a time, three interleaved bytes in and out, which
the compiler cannot vectorize, and grayscale's rounding was a call into the
C library for every pixel (36% of the filter's instructions). The red,
green and blue samples of sixteen pixels are now handed to the mapping as
three float arrays, so the same float operations run in the same order as
vector code, and the rounding is written without the library call. Its
result equals the old expression for every float value, the halves and
NaN included. Grayscale at 800x533 went from 1.76 to 1.17 ms and
`saturation(1.2)` from 2.03 to 1.34 ms; at 2400x1600, in the same row
bands as before, from 5.5 to 3.7 ms and from 7.0 to 3.8 ms. Alpha is
carried through untouched. Responses are unchanged.
