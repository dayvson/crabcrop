A source tagged with an sRGB profile is no longer converted to sRGB (issue
#662). The conversion skipped only a profile byte-equal to the built-in sRGB
profile, which no camera, phone or editor writes, so every still tagged sRGB
ran the whole transform at full size during decode, and all the transform
did was move some colors by one level of rounding. The profile's own
conversion is now asked first: it converts about 5,000 probe colors (a
17-step grid of the RGB cube and the 256 grays), and when none of them moves
by more than one level, an 8-bit source keeps the pixels it decoded to. On a
2400x1600 frame the color step takes 0.6 ms where it took 7.9 ms. Output
changes for these sources and for no others. As it leaves the decoder a
pixel can differ from the previous release's by one level per channel: the
two common profiles, the 3,144-byte sRGB IEC61966-2.1 and the 588-byte one
lcms writes, moved 1.7% and 2.7% of all 8-bit colors, each by one level. The
operations and the encoder then carry that difference into the render, and
can make more of it. Of 14,066 test renders, 132 changed, all of one photo
tagged sRGB IEC61966-2.1. Of its 67 changed PNG renders, 36 differ by one
level at most, in at most 1.5% of their samples; 26, most of them resized,
padded or cropped, by 2 or 3 levels; and the 5 that sharpen by 5 to 14
levels. A lossy render is encoded again from the new frame, so it differs
like a second encode of nearly the same picture (57.7 dB between the old and
the new JPEG at the default quality, 44.5 dB for WebP). Every other profile
converts to the bytes it always has: Display P3, Adobe RGB, Rec. 2020,
ProPhoto, sRGB's primaries under a gamma 2.2 curve or around another white
point, and any profile with a lookup table. So does a 16-bit source,
whatever its profile, because the 16-bit transform of some sRGB profiles
moves a sample by more than one 8-bit level. `keep_profile`, CMYK and gray
profiles and unparseable profiles behave as before. A source that converts
pays for the question: about 20 microseconds when it is 8-bit RGB, and one
more transform setup (about 0.6 ms) when it is 8-bit with alpha. The cache
epoch this change needs is the one the JPEG encoder of issue #668 opens, and
the entry for that epoch names this change too.
