A JPEG source whose first operation is a crop to a fixed alignment is now decoded at reduced scale before the crop (issue #785). Where the first operation is a crop to the center, an edge or a corner — the alignments that place the window from the image dimensions alone — libjpeg-turbo decodes the source at a half, a quarter or an eighth of its size, the smallest frame that still leaves the crop window at least one and a half times its target on both axes, and the crop and its resize finish from that frame, so the full-size pixels are never made. On the benchmark's 2400x1600 sources a `crop:400x300` decodes at a half; measured over 75 crops of the 25 sources (three alignments each), the image handed to the encoder is 56.49 dB PSNR from the full-size crop's at the median and 53.59 dB at worst, SSIMULACRA2 94.68 at worst, well inside no visible change. The pixels differ slightly, which the cache epoch issue #793 opens covers. Every other request decodes as before: a smart, face or object crop, which reads the pixels to place its window; `fit-in` and `pad`; and any crop that does not shrink the source far enough. The scaled decode stands down for any file the two decoders might read differently, as it does for a plain resize, and the full-size crop then answers with the pixels or the error it always gave. No upgrade-corpus crop triggers it — `crab-photo.jpg` is 1000x667, too small for a `crop:400x300` or `crop:300x300` to leave a one-and-a-half-times window at a half — so no corpus entry is re-baselined for this change.
