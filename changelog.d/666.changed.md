Video output composites its frames while the encoder runs (issue #666).
MP4 and WebM output built every frame's sample before it started the
encoder, compositing the frame over black and fitting it to the even
canvas one pixel at a time, and kept all the samples beside the frames
until the encoder was done: a second copy of the animation, 341 MB for 200
frames of 800x533. The thread that feeds the encoder now composites each
frame as its turn comes, a row at a time, into one buffer it reuses, and
writes an opaque frame of the canvas size from where it lies. A 12-frame
480x320 MP4 went from 93.1 to 84.1 ms and 48 frames of 800x533 from 361 to
284 ms. The bytes the encoder is sent are the same, so the video is. The
encoder's timeout (`CRABCROP_VIDEO_TIMEOUT_SECS`) now covers the
compositing as well, which used to run before the encoder started; the
argument list, the output cap, the limits on the encoder process and the
two-encode gate are as they were. In the library,
`crabcrop::video::VideoEncoder::encode` takes its frames by value, as a
`Vec<VideoFrame>`. Responses are unchanged.
