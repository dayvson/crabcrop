`filters:rotate(90)` turns a large frame three times faster (issue #661). The clockwise quarter turn was the one rotation the filter still left to the image crate's loop, which writes each pixel a whole output row away from the last and misses the cache on almost every one. The filter now takes the turn that issue #648 added for EXIF orientations 5 and 6, which writes sixteen output rows at a time: `rotate(90)` of a 2400x1600 photo went from 12.9 to 4.2 ms, on the calling thread as before. The rotation is a permutation of the pixels, so responses are unchanged. Frames under four million samples and 16-bit frames keep the image crate's loop, as they do for `rotate(180)` and `rotate(270)`.
