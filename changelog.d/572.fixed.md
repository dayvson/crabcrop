A disk tier that stops answering, such as a network or FUSE mount that hangs,
no longer hangs requests, purges or upload overwrites (issue #572, part 2 of
#545). The disk tier runs at most 32 operations at once, and nothing limited
how long a request waited for one. Once 32 were stuck in the kernel, every
disk lookup, write-through, memory hit that added a cache group, purge, group
purge and upload overwrite waited forever, while `/health` kept answering
`200`. A request now waits at most 5 seconds in all for disk lookups, its wait
for a disk slot included, then goes on to the bucket or renders. It waits at
most 1 second for its own disk write, and only for an entry the disk does not
hold yet. A memory hit no longer waits on the disk. Purges and upload
overwrites remove the disk tier's index records at once, so the entry stops
serving and the count stays exact, then wait for the files to go while that
makes progress: on a healthy disk they are gone when the purge answers, as
before, unless another purge's deletions are queued first. Otherwise the purge
answers once none of its deletions has finished for 1 second, at most 30
seconds in all, and they go on in the background. Cache groups learned on a
hit are recorded in the index at once, and background jobs, up to 8 at once
and one per entry, write them into the entry files and delete purged files.
Copies of an entry no longer lose a cache group when a stall makes lookups,
writes and group merges overlap, or when two disk hits for one entry run at
once, so a group purge after the disk recovers reaches every copy served under
the group. A disk lookup that runs out of time, a disk write skipped for want
of a disk slot, and a purge that stops waiting for its deletions on a stuck
disk each count in
`crabcrop_cache_tier_errors_total{tier="disk"}` and are logged, at most one
line every 10 seconds. A background job that fails is retried, counts there
too, and is logged at the same rate. `/metrics` also shows
`crabcrop_cache_disk_ops_in_flight`, `crabcrop_cache_disk_groups_behind` and
`crabcrop_cache_disk_deletions_queued`. Boot still waits on the disk: it
writes a probe file and reads every entry header before the server listens. A
shutdown gives the disk tier's background jobs what is left of its grace and
names what they left. A restart before they finish loses some of their work: a
purged file whose deletion is still queued is indexed again at the next boot,
so send that purge again (with a shared tier, the purge's tombstone, or that of
a later purge of the same target that replaces it, stays in force until its
files are deleted, however long that takes, while it is among the newest 1,024
tombstones, the most a refresh reads; the older ones now count in
`crabcrop_cache_shared_tombstones_unread`), and an entry file that had not
caught up with its cache groups comes back without them, so a purge of a
missing group does not reach it. That includes a file a group purge removed
before the file had caught up with the group: purge its URL. Response bytes
are unchanged.
