An 8-bit blur sums a chunk of samples at a time (issue #660). The in-tree
gaussian blur walked the kernel for each output sample, as the image
crate's loop does, so the compiler emitted scalar code for a filter that is
all inner loop. For 8-bit samples both passes now add the terms one kernel
weight at a time over a chunk of 256 samples: the same integer sums in
another order, so the blur stays byte for byte the image crate's. `blur`
at 800x533 went from 10.5 to 8.1 ms at sigma 8 and from 3.4 to 2.8 ms at
sigma 2.5, and the blurred fill of `pad:1200x630:blur` from 165 to
112 ms, in the same row bands on the same threads. Region and face blurs
and pad backgrounds take the same passes. 16-bit and float samples keep
their loops, and the deadline is still checked before every row. Responses
are unchanged.
