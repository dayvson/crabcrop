# Benchmark results: 2026-11-15

crabcrop vs Thumbor vs imgproxy, measured with the harness in
this directory (`benchmark/bench.py`, setup by
`benchmark/setup.sh`). This run refreshes the 2026-11-06
results against crabcrop 0.13.0 (the November 6 run measured
0.9.9) with the same fixtures, operations, and method, so the
two runs read as one series. The headline is cold latency:
the first request for each source, which is the work a
deployment actually pays before any cache or CDN in front of
it can help. Warm and cache figures are recorded for series
continuity in a shorter section near the end. Raw data:
`2026-11-15.json` (comparison) and `2026-11-15-cache.json`
(cache scenarios) beside this file. Method, URL mappings,
and configuration are documented in `benchmark/README.md`
and `benchmark/configs/`; this document records the
environment, the numbers, and the limits.

How this run was produced, and where it differs from
November 6:

- Same repetition scheme: the comparison ran four times for
  crabcrop (two full runs, two crabcrop-only runs) and twice
  in full for Thumbor and imgproxy. Every table cell is the
  median across those runs; the raw JSON preserves each
  individual run under `runs`. The cache scenarios ran
  twice; both runs are in the raw JSON.
- The competitor builds are unchanged from the November 6
  run: the same imgproxy binary, reused bit for bit (its
  checksum matches the artifact the earlier runs measured),
  and a fresh venv at the same Thumbor pin. The pins stay at
  imgproxy 3.31.4 and Thumbor 7.8.0 for series
  comparability. Newer competitor releases exist (see the
  note at the end) and were not measured.
- crabcrop was rebuilt from the v0.13.0 tag. Between 0.9.9
  and 0.13.0, crabcrop shipped a performance wave across
  the delivery, pipeline, encode, and decode paths, moved
  JPEG encoding to libjpeg-turbo and scaled JPEG decoding
  to libjpeg-turbo as well, and updated the JPEG XL encoder
  library (jixel 0.5.5, which produces smaller files at
  slightly lower fidelity at the same nominal quality).
  The byte table below shows those encoder changes; the
  latency tables show the rest.
- The extracted libvips module symlink described in the
  November 6 notes was lost when this VM was replaced
  mid-session and was restored and re-verified before the
  reported runs (without it, imgproxy reports JPEG XL and
  AVIF as unsupported formats; with it, the unsupported
  pattern matches November 6 exactly). Two earlier attempts
  at the full comparison were discarded: a release build
  was compiling on the same host during both, inflating
  every system's numbers three to five fold. The reported
  runs were made in a quiet window (load average about 0.5
  to 1.7 on 4 cores) and agree with each other closely.
- The imgproxy fidelity caveat from October stands: this
  is an official source build against libvips 8.15.1,
  while the official Docker image carries its own newer
  libvips, so imgproxy numbers are imgproxy 3.31.4 on
  libvips 8.15.1, not the Docker image.

## Reading the cold results, including the losses

1. crabcrop is fastest or tied on eight of the ten cold
   operations. On November 6 it won none of the head-to-head
   cold rows against imgproxy except the ones imgproxy does
   not support; this time it leads on resize (18.3 against
   18.9, a tie inside any honest noise band), WebP at all
   three quality points, and JPEG at both quality points,
   and it remains the only one of the three that encodes
   AVIF here and one of two that can grayscale.
2. The crop-fill gap mostly closed. crabcrop crop-fill went
   from 58.5 ms p50 on November 6 to 18.5, a 3.2x
   improvement from the performance wave's pipeline and
   delivery work. imgproxy still wins the row (12.4)
   because libvips scales and crops in one demand-driven
   pass, but the gap is now 1.5x, not 4.8x.
3. Plain JPEG flipped. crabcrop JPEG q60 went from 22.6 to
   13.6 and q95 from 23.6 to 13.9, passing imgproxy (17.9
   and 19.9) on both. That is the libjpeg-turbo encode and
   scaled decode work landing in the rows it touches;
   grayscale, which shares the JPEG decode and encode path,
   improved with it (25.3 to 15.4).
4. JPEG XL flipped the other way, and it is a real
   regression on this row. crabcrop went from 34.0 to 55.2
   while imgproxy held at 38.4. The same release updated
   the JPEG XL encoder library (jixel 0.5.5): output on
   this fixture dropped from 13,175 to 11,445 bytes, 13%
   smaller, at slightly lower fidelity. The trade was taken
   for the rate-distortion curve, not for speed, and on
   this host the speed cost is larger than the November
   measurements of that library suggested. Recorded as a
   loss, not explained away.
5. AVIF is flat: 113.2 against 112.4 on November 6, inside
   noise. The AVIF tile parallelism that shipped in this
   release does not move this fixture at its default
   settings.
6. Output bytes: Thumbor and imgproxy byte counts are
   identical to November 6 in every shared cell, which
   cross-checks the comparison again. crabcrop bytes moved
   in most cells, as expected from the encoder changes:
   JPEG rows slightly smaller (resize 26,540 to 26,241;
   q95 62,716 to 61,635), JPEG XL notably smaller (above),
   AVIF slightly larger (13,772 to 14,203), WebP within a
   percent in both directions. At the same nominal quality
   the three encoders still produce different sizes in both
   directions; the settings are matched by parameter, not
   by measured quality, exactly as in the earlier runs.

## Warm latency and cache behavior (secondary)

In production a CDN usually sits in front of any of these
systems, so a built-in result cache mostly serves the
traffic the CDN lets through; cold performance is the
number that decides what a deployment pays. The warm
figures are kept for series continuity.

Warm p50 (immediate repeat) is 0.2 to 0.3 ms for crabcrop
on every operation, unchanged. Thumbor repeats cost what
its cold requests cost (no result cache in this
configuration); imgproxy repeats likewise. Warm resize
throughput at 8 workers: crabcrop median 1,670 req/s
(four runs: 2,075, 1,746, 1,527, 1,594), Thumbor 25.5,
imgproxy 60.9. November 6 measured a crabcrop median of
1,918 with runs spanning 1,846 to 2,185; the two medians
are inside the same host-variance band and no change is
claimed in either direction.

Cache scenarios (`benchmark/bench_cache.py`, crabcrop
only, two runs; full data in `2026-11-15-cache.json`):

| Measurement | Run 1 | Run 2 | 2026-11-06 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Churn fill, 1,200 distinct URLs, all misses, p50 / mean | 8.8 / 9.2 ms | 8.7 / 9.0 ms | 17.2 / 17.8 ms |
| Rotation at 8 workers, all misses, 2 passes | 122 and 116 req/s | 144 and 145 req/s | 72.6 req/s |
| Cache-disabled fill mean (same 1,200 URLs) | 14.9 ms | 9.0 ms | 17.8 ms |
| Cache bookkeeping per small entry (RSS beyond payload) | 1,952 B (1,810 B grouped) | 1,868 B (1,271 B grouped) | about 1.6 to 1.9 KB |
| Group purge of 3,000 entries | 3.8 ms | 4.6 ms | 3.6 to 3.8 ms |
| 4,500 purges of never-stored targets, RSS growth | 64 KiB | 20 KiB | 24 to 32 KiB |

The churn pattern is unchanged from November 6 (the
byte-bounded LRU holds only the most recent slice of a
working set larger than the budget, and repeat passes over
a scanned set get nothing; hits are rare under churn and
misses are not made slower by the turnover). What changed
is the cost of a miss: a first render under constant
eviction now costs about half what it did, and the
all-miss rotation sustains roughly twice the request rate,
in line with the cold latency gains above. The memory
accounting is unchanged in shape: bookkeeping is per
entry, not per byte, at roughly 1.3 to 2.0 KB per small
entry, and purge tombstones stay capped.

## Competitor release note

The pins did not move for this run. For the record:
imgproxy has since released a v4 line (latest v4.0.24,
published 2026-11-12), which among other changes fixes
ICO loader vulnerabilities fixed in 4.0.16 and 4.0.17;
Thumbor's latest release is still 7.8.0 (2026-05-30), so
its pin is current. A future refresh should decide whether
the series moves to imgproxy v4 or keeps 3.31.4 as the
comparison point; this run keeps the established pins so
the numbers compare against October and November 6.

## Method notes and limits

- Same method as the earlier runs: local origin, cold is
  the first request per source, warm is the immediate
  repeat, one 8-worker warm-throughput pass on resize,
  services measured alone and sequentially, fresh
  connection per request, 25 samples per operation per
  run. Tables report medians across repeated runs.
- All three run with signature verification effectively
  open (crabcrop and Thumbor unsafe mode, imgproxy without
  key or salt). This measures processing, not HMAC.
- Configurations are the committed files in
  `benchmark/configs/`. Thumbor runs at its defaults (no
  result storage). imgproxy runs with `IMGPROXY_WORKERS=4`
  per its docs sizing guidance and with loopback sources
  allowed so it can fetch the local origin at all.
- This host is shared. The reported runs were made in a
  quiet window; two earlier attempts made while a release
  build compiled on the same host were discarded, as
  recorded above. Medians across runs are the mitigation;
  treat small deltas as noise anyway. Rankings are the
  robust part of a run on this host; absolute latencies
  move with the host.
- Single host topology: client, origin, and services share
  one small VM and its loopback. Absolute numbers include
  loopback and client overhead. Rankings can move on
  bigger hardware or with multi-process deployments.
- The cache scenarios measure crabcrop alone, on loopback,
  with the default disk and shared tiers off: they
  describe the in-memory tier's behavior, which is the
  tier the budget configures.
